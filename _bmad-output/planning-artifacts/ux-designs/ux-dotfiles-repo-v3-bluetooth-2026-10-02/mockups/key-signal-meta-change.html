Why hover, and not a permanent text

A dBm figure is a diagnostic, not a status. Nobody opens a Bluetooth list to ask "is my headphones at −52 or −61"; they open it to ask "are they connected and can I connect them." The banded glyph answers that question pre-attentively, at a glance, which is why it is the thing you see. The exact number answers a second, much rarer question — "why does audio keep dropping?" — and it is worth exactly as much attention as that question is worth.

The tooltip is also the only place a number of this kind belongs on this surface: it costs zero vertical space, so a six-device list still fits inside maxContentHeight 420. A permanent −52 dBm on every row costs a line's worth of reading and buys nothing.

BlueZ reports RSSI 0 for a device it has not seen — asleep, or out of range. That reading is not "extremely weak"; it is "no reading at all." So this row renders no signal glyph (there is no signal to draw), which means it has no tooltip, which means the number is not available in any channel whatsoever. If the number were the row's only signal affordance, this row would have nothing to say. The word Not in range is not decoration — it is the sole carrier of the state, reinforced by the 0.45 opacity and the insensitive Connect, which is the two-channel rule satisfied three ways.

This is also the argument against the alternative reading of decision #2 — "just drop the number, the bars are enough." Dropping the number is right because it duplicates the bars. Dropping the word layer would be wrong, because on rssi = 0 rows the bars do not exist. The change moves the number; it does not thin the row.

Verdict · signal-verdict
Gained
One encoding per fact. The row said "strong signal" twice, in two visual registers, at two different scales, in the smallest type on the surface. Now it says it once.
Wi-Fi parity, exactly. WifiRow leads with .settings-net-signal and prints Saved / Secured / Open network underneath. Bluetooth's row is now the same sentence, so the two lists read as one system rather than two dialects.
The row stops competing with itself. A four-bar glyph and a six-digit number sit on the same 10.5 px line at 0.72 opacity; the eye has to arbitrate between them. One glyph, one word — nothing to arbitrate.
New information enters the row. Paired / Ready to pair / Not in range say something the shipped row never said. The removed line was pure redundancy; the added line is not.
The number stays reachable and stays exact. It was a rounded band with a tooltip attached; now it is the tooltip's whole content. Nothing was downgraded to an approximation.
Lost — and stated honestly
The number is no longer visible without a hover. This is a real loss, not a presentational one. Someone scanning for "is this thing at −52 or −88" no longer gets the answer at a glance, and hovering is not free — it costs a deliberate act and a dwell.
It cannot be read while the list is moving. During a scan or a connect the rows re-render; a number you can only get by hovering is a number you cannot get mid-glance. Mitigated only by the fact that the interesting moments are stationary states.
The signal band becomes the whole visible answer. Four buckets is coarse: −52 and −59 are both "high". Anyone who genuinely needs the precision must now ask for it.
Why the loss is acceptable. The band was always the thing actually being read — the digits sat next to it as decoration for a diagnostic question most sessions never ask. Losing the always-visible copy costs the rare reader one hover and saves the common reader a redundant line, on the one surface where the user is already comparing Bluetooth to the Wi-Fi list directly above it. If that trade is wrong, it is wrong in a way this page makes visible — which is what it is for.

If the owner reverses this, the fallback is not "put the number back" — it is the third option this page makes visible for free: keep the word layer, keep the tooltip, and print the number only on the selected / connected row, where attention is already focused and `Disconnect` is one click away. That is a divergence from WifiRow, so it would need its own parity row in key-parity.html. It is offered as an explicit escape hatch, not as a recommendation.
