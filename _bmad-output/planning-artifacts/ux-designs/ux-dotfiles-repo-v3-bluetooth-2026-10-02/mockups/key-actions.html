Why Can take up to 30 s is a fixed string

The watchdog is 30 s. A countdown would be the better sentence — but it needs a timer running in the view, and the view is a strictly thin reader. bluetoothBusy() carries no remaining time, so a countdown would be a second, unsourced source of truth for a number the service already owns. The static line says the same thing ("this takes a while, and I am not going to pretend to know when it ends") at a fraction of the architectural cost. It is one line of copy, not a widget.

Single-flight — anyBusy()

runAction returns early when bluetoothBusy().address !== null. So while one row works, every other row's primary action is insensitive — otherwise the second click would look accepted and then silently do nothing. Unpair goes further than insensitive: it is hidden entirely, because a visible dead control is a trap and a half-visible row action pair reads as a bug. The service being single-flight is not an implementation detail to hide; it is the behaviour the surface has to show.

Why inline and not a modal

The popup is a surface with no modal stack, and a modal would grab a keyboard the surface otherwise does not hold — the exact thing Keymode.NONE exists to avoid. An inline confirm in the existing action slot is one row: no new window, no reflow, no focus trap. Keep sits beside Unpair device, so the destructive path is one click longer than the safe one — the only asymmetry that matters — and the confirm is not colour-alone: the button says Unpair device in words. Opening a confirm on one row closes any other open confirm; the popup's Esc / click-outside dismisses an open confirm before the surface closes.

Set(Powered) rejected. The switch sprang back to its previous value and the body collapsed to the one-line off state. The string tells him the truth is outside the panel — rfkill, a BIOS switch, a policy — which is exactly the class of problem he cannot fix from here and would otherwise debug blind.
Bluetooth
No Bluetooth adapter available
No Adapter1 on the bus. The switch is insensitive — visibly so — and the real settings-panel/info-caution glyph carries the line, reusing the Wi-Fi weak-security precedent. Never a crash, never a dead-looking-but-clickable switch.

Every one of these strings is produced by the service and printed by the view without edits. That is the whole feedback contract: one line, at the top of the body, in the caution slot, with no timer, no toast and no dismissal step. Nothing in the popup below the message line moves when a failure arrives — the row simply stops spinning.
